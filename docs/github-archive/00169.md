Eighty labelled pairs, three judges, identical inputs. Data and per-pair results are committed alongside the write-up.

| Scorer | Precision | Recall | F1 | Accuracy | ms/pair | $/1k |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MiniCheck | **1.000** | 0.967 | **0.983** | **0.988** | 332 | 0 |
| JEV | 0.938 | **1.000** | 0.968 | 0.975 | **191** | 0.023 |
| GLiNER | 0.400 | 0.667 | 0.500 | **0.500** | 1445 | 0 |

**GLiNER is at chance** — it accepted 30 of the 50 unsupported pairs and rejected a third of the supported ones. That matches its published judge benchmark and it is also the slowest.

**MiniCheck raised no false alarms** (0 of 50 unsupported accepted). **JEV found every supported pair** and missed two hard negatives.

The open question was whether the safeguard's 20/20 failure rate came from the judge being unable to tell support. It does not: both working judges judge correct pairs correctly. GLiNER should not be used as a support judge.

Pairs are labelled by construction (sentence with its own passage = supported; with another case's passage = unsupported; with a year altered = hard negative), so the labels do not depend on a labeller's opinion. Caveats are in the README — constructed pairs are easier than real answers, and the hard negatives are not all genuinely unsupported.
